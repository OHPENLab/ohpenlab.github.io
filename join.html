Open positions

Postdoctoral researchers — agentic AI and reinforcement learning

We have multiple funded postdoctoral positions building agentic AI and reinforcement-learning systems for healthcare delivery. Each position is attached to an active NIH or industry project, with real clinical deployment as the goal rather than a benchmark number. Start dates are flexible.

NLM/NIH R01 · DischargeWatch

Next-day hospital discharge

Agentic and reinforcement-learning methods over structured EHRs, clinical notes, and audit logs to predict next-day discharge and to sequence the actions that actually unblock it. Includes silent model testing in a simulation environment and value-sensitive design with interest-holder advisory panels.

NLM/NIH R01 · Interaction networks

Repairing healthcare team structure

Fine-tuned LLMs that extract clinically meaningful EHR tasks, graph neural networks that detect and explain inefficient nodes, edges, and subnetworks, and role-specific agents that propose and evaluate repairs to how care teams are organized — with humans in the loop throughout.

Eli Lilly · R2FI

Alzheimer's care pathway optimization

Sequential decision-making across the early Alzheimer's pathway, from biomarker confirmation to first infusion. Work spans event-and-timestamp extraction, a triage agent, an MRI safety agent, and a generative prior-authorization agent deployed in routine VUMC workflows.

Eli Lilly · Continuous obesity care

Chronic care pathway optimization

Learning policies that keep patients in continuous obesity care over months and years, combining longitudinal EHR data, wearables, and patient-reported outcomes with role-specific agents representing patient, clinician, and health-system perspectives.

NLM/NIH R01 · Drug interactions

Biomedical knowledge discovery

Graph AI and foundation models over knowledge graphs linking drugs, genes, proteins, diseases, adverse events, multi-omics, and single-cell data — for drug safety, drug repurposing, and therapeutic target discovery, validated against real-world clinical evidence.

What we look for

A PhD (or imminent completion) in computer science, biomedical informatics, electrical and computer engineering, statistics, or a related field. Strong programming and a track record of peer-reviewed publication. Depth in one or more of: reinforcement learning, LLM agents and tool use, graph neural networks, foundation models, causal inference, or human-centered AI evaluation. Direct experience with EHR data is welcome but not required — we will teach it.

How to apply

Email you.chen@vanderbilt.edu with “Postdoc application — OHPEN Lab” in the subject line. Include your CV, a one-page research statement naming the area above that interests you most, up to three representative papers, and contact information for three references. Applications are reviewed on a rolling basis.

Open positions

PhD students

Our PhD students work on projects at the intersection of AI and healthcare: human-centered, multimodal, and agentic AI for healthcare delivery (clinical pathway optimization, discharge prediction, care-team network analysis from EHR audit logs) and graph AI and foundation models for drug safety, drug repurposing, and therapeutic discovery (knowledge graphs, graph neural networks, large language models, pharmacogenetics with biobank-scale data). Students publish in venues such as KDD, JAMA Network Open, JAMIA, and Clinical Pharmacology & Therapeutics.

What we look for

A strong foundation in machine learning, network/graph analysis, data mining, large-scale data analysis, or statistics — and the discipline and motivation to pursue independent research in computer science, engineering, or biomedical informatics.

Where you'd apply

Apply to the PhD programs in Computer Science, Electrical and Computer Engineering, or Biomedical Informatics at Vanderbilt, and name Dr. You Chen as a potential advisor.

How to reach out

Email you.chen@vanderbilt.edu with your CV, transcripts, a brief statement of research interests, and (if available) representative publications or code.
